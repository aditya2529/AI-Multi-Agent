# Director Pitch — Round 2

**Audience:** Senior Director (had own SDLC idea) + Other Director (asked about tokens)
**Length:** 90 seconds to read out loud
**Tone:** Confident, not defensive. Acknowledge their points first.

---

## Opening (15 seconds)

> "Sir, I heard you the first time, and I went back and worked on both points you raised. I want to give you 90 seconds — not to defend my idea, but to show you what I changed because of your feedback."

---

## Point 1 — To the Senior Director (30 seconds)

> "You said you already have an SDLC automation idea. That's great news — it means we're aligned on the *problem*. What I've built is an *implementation* of that idea, ready today. Eight AI agents that already work like a Scrum team. Demo evidence is recorded.
>
> I'm not proposing a competing idea. I'm proposing the engine that runs your vision. You own the strategy. I bring the working product. Let's combine them."

---

## Point 2 — To the Other Director (30 seconds)

> "You asked the right question — *is this cost-efficient enough for an enterprise client?* My first answer would have been hand-wavy. So I went back and audited the platform for token waste.
>
> Today, the platform burns about 290,000 tokens per sprint. I've identified 10 places where we use AI to do things that plain Python code can do — checking rules, counting lines, parsing JSON. By moving that logic into code, we cut token usage by **~90%**. Per sprint cost drops from $4.50 to about $0.40.
>
> For a client like Citi running 100 sprints a month, that's $450 to $40. Same output, 10% of the cost.
>
> I have the report ready. I'd like 10 minutes of your time to walk through it."

---

## Close (15 seconds)

> "Both points you raised made the product better. I'm not asking you to bet on my idea — I'm asking for the chance to show you the working version of *your* idea, at enterprise cost.
>
> If 10 minutes doesn't convince you, I drop it. Fair?"

---

## If They Push Back

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| *"How do you know the 90% number is real?"* | "I can run the same demo before and after the fix and show the token meter on both. Side-by-side proof." |
| *"Why are you building this and not the platform team?"* | "I built it on my own time because I saw the gap. I'd rather hand it to the platform team than own it alone." |
| *"What if my idea is different from yours?"* | "Then your idea wins. My code is the substrate — your strategy decides what it builds." |
| *"This sounds too good to be true."* | "It is real. 95 unit tests pass. Demo is recorded. I'm not selling slides — I'm showing working software." |

---

## Three Things to Carry With You

1. **Acknowledge first, pitch second.** Both directors felt unheard. Fix that before anything else.
2. **Numbers, not adjectives.** "90% cheaper" and "$40 vs $450" land harder than "very efficient".
3. **Offer to demo, not to explain.** Working software beats slides. Always.
